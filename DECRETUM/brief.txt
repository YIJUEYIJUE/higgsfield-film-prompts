[DECRETUM] is a cinematic science-fiction mystery about a mysterious eyeless boy who sets out on a journey to discover who he really is.

Raised in a remote village despite being different from everyone around him, the boy grows up feeling that he does not truly belong. Haunted by questions about his identity

 his origin, and the strange world surrounding him, he eventually leaves the only home he has ever known.

Guided by a small one-eyed companion, he enters a vast unknown world filled with ancient landscapes, mysterious creatures, forgotten civilizations, and supernatural phenomena.

His journey is not only about discovering the world around him — it is about discovering himself.

✨ Context
The project exists to explore a simple but powerful question:

What if the thing you spend your entire life trying to understand about yourself is the very thing that determines your destiny?

The film is built around the idea of identity versus destiny.

Hero has never chosen to be different. He does not know where he came from, why he is different, or why the world seems to react to him with fear and rejection.

His greatest fear is that he is destined to remain unknown.

But as he travels deeper into the unknown, he begins to discover that perhaps his lack of identity is not a curse — perhaps it is the beginning of something much larger.

The mystery gradually shifts from:

“Who is he?”

Audience

A cinematic audience interested in:

science fiction, psychological mystery, fantasy adventure, creature-driven worlds, emotional storytelling, philosophical themes and visually ambitious cinema.

The film is designed to work primarily through visual storytelling, atmosphere, mystery and emotional character moments, rather than exposition.

🎨 Visuals

The creatures featured in the film are inspired by the skeletal anatomy of a chicken, reimagined into original alien lifeforms through an artistic and cinematic interpretation 

The costumes and accessories were designed as an extension of the film’s central idea: the impossibility of breaking the cycle of what has already been written. Repeated circular forms, layered fabrics, spiraling motifs, and coiled metal ornaments create a visual language of repetition and continuity. The characters do not simply wear these elements; they appear bound to them, as if the same pattern governing their world has been woven into their bodies and identities
