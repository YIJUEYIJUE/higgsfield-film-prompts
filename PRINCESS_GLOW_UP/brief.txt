This is a project brief for the series "Princess glow up"

Logline: A bare-faced scholarship girl at an elite academy where every student dresses like a runway is invisible until the day she isn't — and the moment the school finally looks at her, so does the one boy she never wanted to be seen by.

About the project 

"Princess Glow Up" is a short drama series about a plain, painfully shy student inside a school where fashion is the social currency and everyone is dressed like an editorial. Its world is a single pastel palace of a school — a mint-and-gold cafeteria with orange lunch trays, a chandeliered marble atrium with twin staircases, corridors where students pass in coquette-doll and Y2K couture — plus one ordinary bedroom that grounds the whole thing in real life. The visual language stays consistent throughout: soft daylight, a pastel palette of peach, powder blue, cream and gold, low contrast, natural skin texture with nothing smoothed away, and a camera that stays close and calm so a shy glance is allowed to land. The drama is deliberately small-scale — a hidden watch, a business card slid across a lunch table, a friend calling your name across a hall — and every shot is tuned until the beat it carries earns its place.

Reference assets

Every character, prop and location in the series is locked to an actual reference photo — a three-view turntable of Enju bare-faced in her oversized hoodie, a second turntable of her styled glow-up look, Ryan in his black monogram cardigan, Chen in both of his looks, Jenny in both of hers, a mood board of the female student body, a group shot of Chen's friends, and the locations shot empty: the cafeteria, the atrium, the corridor, the bedroom. Every generated frame that later becomes a continuity anchor gets tagged too — the cafeteria master two-shot, the staircase group blocking — so a returning scene rebuilds from an image rather than from memory. Each is tagged (@image1 through @image19+) and pulled by tag into any prompt that needs it, with an explicit instruction never to re-describe or expand the environment beyond what the photo already shows. Location drift turned out to be the biggest single risk in this project: given a pastel palace, the model's instinct is to make it grander — adding chandeliers, widening the hall, re-plating school food as restaurant food — and the location lock is what stops it. The second biggest was wardrobe: with this many maximalist outfits on screen at once, an unlocked background extra will quietly borrow a named character's silhouette.

For video prompts we write our system prompts in a Claude chat loaded with a custom Seedance 2.5 prompt-building skill, alongside the full script, so every prompt stays in-context with the plot — a shot knows what beat it's serving, what just happened, and what the character is feeling in that moment. For additional consistency, a stable "Style Prefix" and "Constraints" paragraph opens every prompt and stays fixed across the whole project.

Style Prefix:

Soft K-drama pastel look — bright diffused daylight, palette of peach, powder 
blue, cream, gold and pink marble, gentle low contrast, natural skin texture 
with visible pores and no beauty smoothing, very light grain, unhurried 
stillness. 50mm lens feel, soft background bokeh. Naturalistic, intimate. 
Steady, level, well-composed framing — flat true horizon, no tilt; the camera 
holds still and lets a moment breathe.

Visual Constraints:

Photoreal live-action — no 3D render, no game-engine gloss, no cartoon or anime read. Hard positive locks in every prompt: headcounts, seating and screen geography, prop states, screen-direction, and wardrobe continuity are written into the prompt, not left to chance. Faces blink and breathe, never masklike; identities, hair, glasses and wardrobe match their @tag references every shot — Enju's round frames and Ryan's rectangular frames never swap. Locations render only what the location reference shows and are never widened, re-furnished or made grander; the cafeteria stays a school canteen with orange compartment trays inside a grand hall, never a restaurant. Background students stay in soft focus inside their own conversations and never look at camera. Enju's face stays bare and unretouched until the glow-up; after it, the styling is the only thing that changes about her. Ryan's watch stays an ordinary object in ordinary light — no glint, no bloom, no push-in. Slow motion appears only where scripted and returns to full speed on a hard cut, never a ramp. Dialogue is only the exact scripted lines, one voice at a time, delivered at ordinary conversational pace with real pauses. 4K UHD, sharp clarity, stable lighting, consistent frame rate, clean picture.

It is important to include audio constraints such as "No music. Only SFX." to only have sound effects in our shots — fog-muffled ambience, a single distant rifle crack, breath, footsteps on wet ground, the low hum of a crowded corridor. Music was created during the post-production stage.

Post-Production: Color, ,Music, Sound

Sound design was treated as an essential storytelling tool. Every track was manually cleaned, refined and enhanced with carefully placed effects — cutlery and trays in the canteen, the low hum of a crowded hall, footsteps crossing marble, the soft rustle of organza — building a sonic space that makes an ordinary school day feel lived-in.
